Struggles

Fatima Jinnah entered public life at a time when few South Asian Muslim women did. In 1947, women in Lahore took part in processions and faced arrests under her leadership. After her brother’s death, she withdrew from active politics for years, and her health and privacy were often a concern. 
profilepk

Her hardest struggle was the 1965 election. She stood against President Ayub Khan, as a candidate of the Combined Opposition Parties. The campaign was short, and the voters were not the general public but a limited electoral college. She faced criticism from religious scholars who held that a woman could not lead an Islamic state, and her campaign reportedly faced attacks on her patriotism and modesty. Ayub Khan’s supporters argued that his government had delivered stability and development.

Key Contributions
Political leadership. She gave a face to women’s participation in the Pakistan Movement and mobilised women for public life.
Refugee relief. She played a significant role in the settlement of the Muhajirs in the new state.

Challenging military rule. The 1965 election drew huge crowds; one train journey of her campaign, the “Freedom Special”, ran 22 hours late because crowds at each station wanted to hear her. Many historians treat this as a landmark moment for democratic politics in Pakistan.

Election result. She lost the electoral college to Ayub Khan, but many historians and journalists have said the election was not free or fair, and that she had strong support, especially in cities such as Karachi and Dhaka. Official accounts at the time accepted Ayub Khan’s victory.

Death and Legacy

She died in Karachi in July 1967. The recorded cause was heart failure. Some commentators have raised questions about the circumstances, but no official finding contradicted the recorded cause, and you may wish to mention that the matter is still debated by some writers.
